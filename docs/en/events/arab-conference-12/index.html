The Arab Conferences for Astronomy and Space Sciences

The 12th Arab Conference on Space and Astronomy Sciences and the 7th Islamic Astronomical Conference

The 12th Arab Conference on Space and Astronomy Sciences and the 7th Islamic Astronomical Conference come twenty years after the founding of the Arab Union for Astronomy and Space Sciences. The Union has grown quickly in recent years and now brings together most Arab scientists in space and astronomy science, research and technology, with more than 500 scientists and researchers among its members. It is one of the specialised unions of the Council of Arab Economic Unity of the League of Arab States, and a member of the International Astronomical Union.

The Union aims to raise the standing and level of astronomy and space science so that they can drive progress and develop Arab society scientifically and technically, and to preserve the Arab and Islamic astronomical heritage and highlight its role in the advance of human civilisation. It studies scientific issues shared by Arab countries and exchanges information and expertise across the basic and applied fields of space and astronomy. It encourages interest in space and astronomy in Arab countries and supports scientific institutions and societies in the Arab world, and helps amateur and professional Arab astronomers set up their own institutions and societies in Arab countries that do not yet have them. It works to determine the beginnings of lunar months scientifically, supported by observation, to unify scientific terminology in space and astronomy in the Arab world, and to encourage research, publishing, writing and translation of scientific material in Arabic. It also advises institutions, bodies and individuals in these fields.

The Arab Conference on Space and Astronomy Sciences began twenty years ago as an initiative of the Union to connect specialists and enthusiasts in these sciences across the Arab countries. After the first five conferences in Amman, the Union's headquarters, the sixth was held in Tripoli, Libya, the seventh in Jordan, the eighth in Djerba, Tunisia, the ninth in Khartoum, Sudan, the tenth in Muscat, Oman, and the eleventh in Sharjah, UAE. The twelfth edition returns to Amman, Jordan, distinguished by the variety of its events and exhibitions and by the contributions of international experts with long and varied experience, helping the conference succeed and encouraging regional institutions to play a growing role in space and astronomy.
