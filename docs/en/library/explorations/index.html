Astronomical explorations

Ever since humans first walked the Earth they have looked up in wonder at the stars, planets and other bodies in the sky, trying to explain many phenomena and unravel the mysteries of everyday observations. But with only the human eye to observe with, they could see just a small number of stars and other celestial objects. As observing methods and instruments advanced, astronomers and amateurs became able to explore outer space with ease, from the ground, from the air, and from below and above the atmosphere: optical and radio telescopes, others working in infrared, X-rays and gamma rays, telescopes floating in space and others fixed on the ground…

Many space telescopes send us discoveries about this vast universe, putting the information within everyone's reach. Some were sent for specific purposes, such as studying the Sun, Earth's atmosphere, and planets like Venus, Mercury and Mars, as well as chasing comets, asteroids and exoplanets, and even elementary particles, cosmic rays and much more.

The solar system

A group of celestial bodies that includes the Sun, the planets and their moons, and a large number of objects gravitationally bound to the Sun in elliptical orbits around it.

On 24 August 2006 the International Astronomical Union adopted a definition of a planet that reduced the number of planets to eight. The definition has three main points.

A planet is a celestial body that:

  1. is in orbit around the Sun;
  2. has enough mass for its self-gravity to overcome rigid-body forces so that it assumes hydrostatic equilibrium, a nearly round shape;
  3. has cleared the neighbourhood around its orbit.

A dwarf planet is a celestial body that:

  1. is in orbit around the Sun;
  2. has enough mass for its self-gravity to overcome rigid-body forces so that it assumes hydrostatic equilibrium, a nearly round shape;

but has not cleared the neighbourhood around its orbit. Under this definition Pluto is classed as a dwarf planet.

Exploring the Sun: SOHO, STEREO A & B, ACE. Exploring Mercury: MESSENGER.
Exploring Mars: Mars Express, the Spirit and Opportunity rovers, Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter. Exploring Venus: Venus Express.
Exploring Saturn: Cassini–Huygens. Exploring Uranus: visited by Voyager 2 in 1986; there are no dedicated missions to Uranus.
Exploring Neptune: visited by Voyager 2 in 1989; there are no dedicated missions to Neptune. Dwarf planets: Pluto, Ceres, Eris.

Arab Union for Astronomy and Space Sciences
