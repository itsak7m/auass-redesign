The Accurate Times program

1. The Accurate Times program

Accurate Times is a computer program for calculating prayer times and many other astronomical events. It is used by Jordan's Ministry of Awqaf, Islamic Affairs and Holy Places to calculate prayer times, and by the Islamic Fiqh Academy of the Muslim World League to calculate prayer times in European countries. It runs on Windows and was written by Eng. Mohammad Odeh, head of the Islamic Crescents' Observation Project (ICOP). The program calculates the following:

  1. Prayer times (fajr, sunrise, dhuhr, asr, maghrib and isha).
  2. Sun times (start and end of twilight, sunrise, sunset and transit).
  3. Moon times (moonrise, transit and moonset).
  4. Topocentric and geocentric Moon phases (new moon, first quarter, full moon, last quarter).
  5. Visibility of the new crescent and of the old crescent at the end of the month.
  6. World maps of the visibility of the new and old crescents.
  7. Sun and Moon ephemerides.
  8. The qibla direction.
  9. Qibla time (when the Sun or its shadow points along the qibla).
  10. Conversion between Hijri and Gregorian dates.
  11. Playing the adhan when prayer time comes.
  12. A reminder sound before the next prayer time.

2. Qibla direction

Accurate Times calculates the qibla direction (the direction of the shortest distance to Makkah) and the qibla time, when the Sun is in the direction of the qibla or its shadow points towards it. It also shows the world qibla map, a world map showing the qibla direction everywhere as coloured bands. Below is a small sample of the world qibla map.

3. Converting between Hijri and Gregorian dates

Accurate Times can convert Hijri dates to Gregorian and back. Please note that this conversion uses conventional astronomical calculations, not crescent sighting.

4. Options

Accurate Times offers several options not fully available in any other program:

  • Fajr and isha angles: it is generally agreed that fajr and isha begin when the centre of the Sun's disc is 18 degrees below the horizon, but some countries or organisations prefer other values such as 16, 19 or 21 degrees.
  • Daylight saving time: many countries use summer time, and this option gives correct prayer times in summer.
  • Adding or subtracting minutes: some organisations prefer to add or subtract a few minutes from a particular prayer such as fajr, dhuhr or maghrib.
  • Elevation: sunrise and sunset at high places differ from those at sea level. The Sun rises earlier and sets later at higher places, and the difference between sea level and a place 1,000 m high is between 5 and 13 minutes, depending on latitude and season.
  • Asr prayer: the user can choose the majority method (Shafi'i, Hanbali and Maliki) or the Hanafi method.
  • Display precision: results can be shown to the nearest minute or the nearest second.
  • Language: the program supports Arabic and English, but the help and results files are in English only.

5. Prayer times at high latitudes

When the signs of fajr and isha disappear at high latitudes, Accurate Times can calculate alternative times, based on the method adopted in 2009 by the Muslim World League. To use it, open the "Options" menu and tick "Enable alternative calculations for high latitudes".

6. Prayer alarm

Accurate Times offers two kinds of alarm. The first is a beep repeated several times a set period before prayer time, to remind you to pray before the next prayer is due. The second plays the adhan when prayer time comes; the user can choose the adhan of Makkah or of Al-Quds.

7. Location

  • Refraction: for sunrise most programs use the standard refraction value of 34 arcminutes, but refraction varies with the seasons, depending on temperature and air pressure, and this can change sunrise and sunset by tens of seconds. It is better to enter the actual average temperature and pressure for the location. Standard conditions assume 10 °C and 1,010 millibars, but these are sea-level values; at 1,000 m, for example, standard pressure is 900 millibars!
  • City times: Accurate Times can give prayer times for a city, but which point in the city should be used, the eastern or western parts? This option solves the problem. Suppose you want prayer times for Amman, which is 12 km across: enter the coordinates of the westernmost part of Amman in the "Location" menu, and enter "12" in the "City times" option. Fajr and sunrise will then be calculated for the eastern parts of the city, 12 km east of the original coordinates, while dhuhr, asr, maghrib and isha will be calculated for the western parts.

8. World crescent visibility map

Accurate Times shows the visibility of the crescent everywhere in the world by drawing coloured curves on a world map, each colour showing a level of visibility. Its features include:

  1. Curves for both the new crescent and the old crescent.
  2. Visibility according to three criteria: Odeh, Yallop and the South African Astronomical Observatory (SAAO).
  3. Areas where sighting is impossible: for the new crescent, when the Moon sets before the Sun or topocentric conjunction occurs after sunset; for the old crescent, when the Moon rises after the Sun or topocentric conjunction occurs before sunrise.
  4. Saving the world visibility map as an image with one click.

The colours for each criterion are: a. Odeh criterion:

  1. Blue: the crescent is visible by telescope only.
  2. Pink: visible by telescope, and possibly by naked eye in perfect atmospheric conditions for an experienced observer.
  3. Green: easily visible to the naked eye.

b. Yallop criterion:

  1. Blue: visible by telescope only.
  2. Pink: after it is found with a telescope, it may be seen with the naked eye.
  3. Yellow: visible to the naked eye in perfect conditions.
  4. Green: easily visible to the naked eye.

c. SAAO criterion:

  1. Blue: not visible without a telescope or binoculars; may be visible with optical aid.
  2. Green: visible to the naked eye.

For all criteria:

  1. Red: sighting impossible, when the Moon sets before the Sun or topocentric conjunction occurs after sunset.
  2. No colour: not visible, when conjunction occurs before sunset and the Moon sets after the Sun, but the crescent is not bright enough to be seen even with optical aid.
  3. Light blue: unknown, which happens at high latitudes when the Moon does not set or rise on the day of observation; deciding visibility then needs more calculation than Accurate Times currently provides.

Please note that seeing the crescent at the start of each band depends strongly on the weather, eyesight, the observer's experience and looking directly at the crescent's position.
