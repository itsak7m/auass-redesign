Key

  • Eye Naked eye
  • Eye* Naked eye in good conditions; binoculars may help to find it
  • Optical Binoculars or telescope only
  • Not visible Not visible even with optical aid

How it is computed

Start of the month: by the Umm al-Qura rule: if the conjunction falls before sunset in Makkah on the 29th and the Moon sets after the Sun, the next day is the 1st of the month; otherwise the month completes thirty days. These are astronomical calculations and do not replace the official announcement in each country.

Crescent visibility: by the Odeh (2004) criterion, which uses the Moon's altitude and the crescent's width at the best time after sunset, giving a value V: the crescent is visible to the naked eye if V is 5.65 or more, with the naked eye in good conditions between 2 and 5.65, with binoculars or a telescope only between −0.96 and 2, and not visible below that. A +1 d mark means the Moon was not yet born on the observing evening in that city, so visibility was computed for the following evening.

Tools: the open-source astronomy-engine library (MIT licence) for the positions of the Sun and Moon. Conjunction times are in Makkah time (UTC+3).

The religious ruling on the start of a month rests on sighting the crescent or on what the official authority in each country adopts (Iftaa department, Shari'a court or moon-sighting committee).
