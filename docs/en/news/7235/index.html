Science news · 22 July 2020

The Hope probe in the eyes of the Arabs

Translated from the Arabic original. اقرأ النص الأصلي بالعربية

Egyptian space science experts praised the achievement of the UAE's "Hope Probe" hours before its launch towards Mars, noting that the experiment is a "major qualitative leap" that will push space science in the Arab world to wider horizons. They stressed that the Emirati Mars exploration project places the entire Arab region in an advanced scientific position, because it will be the first true aerial observatory of the Red Planet, besides the thorough study it will provide on the relationship between Mars's climate and its terrain.
Dr. Mohamed Bahaa El-Din Arjoun, professor of spacecraft dynamics and aviation and former director of the Egyptian space programme, praised the "confident calm" of the UAE in managing its ambitious space programme, noting that the way the UAE managed this project could play a great role in localising this modern technology in the Arab region, a task that needs long patience, great support for universities and scientific research centres, as well as ambitious political will.
Dr. Arjoun, nicknamed the "Father of Egyptian space", called on the UAE to share the results of this work with the Arab countries; it lays the foundation for a "great Arab success base", by moving towards building scientific and technological foundations and localising space technology within the Arab world.
Arjoun said the importance of this blessed step is that it will move the Arabs in general from the position of follower of what has been happening on the world stage in this field for decades to the position of participant, even if modestly, noting that this step will open the way for real Arab participation in bigger missions, especially given the expertise the Arab region holds in this field, expertise that contributed to Saudi Arabia, Egypt and Algeria undertaking several space experiments. Arjoun added that the importance of this step lies in the fact that a large part of it is purely Emirati-Arab, which represents a qualitative leap we have the right to rejoice in, and that this umbrella should extend to include other Arab countries, given the value of the experiment and the scientific returns it will bring.
For his part Dr. Yousry Shafik, academic editor of the International Journal of Astronomy and Astrophysics Research, described the new Emirati experiment as an "important step on the road to rooting space technology in the Arab region", noting that space science long received great attention from the Arab region's countries before weak material resources prevented achievements in this field.
Shafik said that probing the depths of space has become a scientific priority in recent times, noting that Egypt has come a long way in this field, which for a long time was limited to some specialised departments in the Faculty of Science at Cairo University and its counterpart at Al-Azhar University, before other universities began establishing specialised departments such as the Faculty of Science at Helwan University and Beni Suef University.
Dr. Rasha Ghoneim, head of the Space Research Laboratory at the National Research Institute of Astronomy and Geophysics, says: the UAE's launch of its probe to Mars is a matter of pride for all Arabs, because in the end it is an achievement that shows the world that Arabs are able to probe the depths of space and take a strong part in making modern human civilisation.
Ghoneim adds: "The success of the pioneering Emirati experiment will create a state of competition among many Arab countries to catch up with the great technological development in this vital field, and it will also provide researchers in various specialisations with much of the information the probe will arrive at, information the UAE will make available to various researchers, by virtue of the real bonds of brotherhood between it and all Arab countries. This participation gains its importance from being pure, between researchers who share the same language and religion, which is very different from what many Western research centres allow, which share what they manage to obtain only within a narrow scope."
Dr. Alaa El-Nahry, Vice President of the UN Regional Centre for Space Science, sees that the UAE's experiment of launching the Hope Probe will provide new evidence of the Arabs' ability to contribute with the required effectiveness to enriching human civilisation and knowledge, noting that the experiment will plant in the conscience of new generations the values of confidence, ambition and ability, the possibility of achievement and success. El-Nahry adds that the experiment is a practical model that will inspire the peoples of the region, especially as it comes at a time when many Arab countries suffer various problems, so the project is in truth a symbol of hope and a creative means to inspire new generations and push them to think with the positivity required for the future.

Jordanian space and astronomy experts told "Al Khaleej" of the importance of launching the "Hope Probe" as an Arab achievement that could change the course of space science globally, not only for its role in discovering the secrets of the planet Mars, but for what the results will reflect on precise scientific research.
Astronomy expert Imad Mujahid, head of the Crescents Department at Jordan's General Iftaa Department, said: "We are facing an achievement that brings the Arabs into history through its widest doors and places us at the heart of the development of space science and among its pioneers. This precedent and great leap is a source of pride and honour for every Arab and lays the foundation for important stages that will change the course of leadership in this field globally."
He added: "The importance of the prominent achievement lies in several directions: an Arab country has managed to break the monopoly on launching related 'space probes' and rely on national Emirati youth efforts, which opens the horizons wide for Arab youth's attention to this field, in addition to building on the exploration results using the latest advanced technology factors."
He continued: "There are many questions about the planet Mars posed by specialists and experts in space science, and great reliance has come to be placed on the 'Hope Probe' to reach answers that lead to more important areas in advanced sciences, including those relating to water and the gaseous envelope, especially the decline of oxygen and hydrogen to low levels, in addition to researching the drop in temperature, the reason for dust storms, the occurrence of ice and so on."
He said: "We are about to rewrite history in space science and we should look at this probe with great hope and wonder at its ability to combine remarkable capabilities of the country's sons with optimal use of the gains of technology, which makes the UAE sit on the throne of technological countries according to a scientific strategy that outperforms major world countries."
Mujahid stressed that investing the ability to keep pace with space science in a way that brings benefit to humanity and discovering natural wealth that serves humanity is consistent with the firm principles of the Emirati leadership, whose benefit has reached the whole world.
Mujahid pointed to a book titled "Intelligent Life on Mars" that he is about to publish soon, in which he devoted a whole chapter to the "Hope Probe" and what the world awaits when it arrives and achieves results in February 2021, coinciding with 50 years since the founding of the state and the establishment of the Union.
Dr. Hanna Sabat, president of the Jordanian Astronomical Society and dean of the Institute of Astronomy and Space Sciences at Al al-Bayt University, said: "The launch of this probe represents an Arab distinction known only to a handful of countries in the world that can be counted on the fingers of one hand, which makes us feel proud and leads us to a high ambition exceeding building satellites to reshaping and reconstituting priorities so that space science is among the foundations of Arab scientific interests that had been limited to others."
He added: "It is not only specialists who are awaiting the probe's path and results, but every Arab today has become part of this achievement, which the Emirati leadership announced would include the Arab world from the beginning and without end. There is no doubt that the data the probe will observe is very important for achieving benefit relating to the atmosphere, climate change and weather conditions, and this is reflected in the development of modern sciences."
Sabat pointed to more than 200 specialised scientific institutions benefiting from the observational data and the possibility of opening channels of cooperation between Arab countries in this context, stimulating student training fields and forming the nucleus of an Arab renaissance in space sciences that gathers expertise and changes the direction of the path.
Dr. Awni Khasawneh, head of the UN Regional Centre for Space Science and Technology Education for Western Asia, said: "Some years ago we were talking about an Arab dream about the possibility of reaching Mars, and today the UAE achieves this dream and announces it as a proud Arab one, in a big step that strengthens hope in the abilities of our Arab nation's sons."
He added: "The UAE has recorded its name with pride and dignity and succeeded, through engineering and technical energies, in achieving a dazzling success in reaching this stage, and leads a prominent role as an important Arab hub. Since the UAE Space Agency was launched, eyes have been on the next steps."
Khasawneh described the "Hope Probe" as a strategic Emirati-Arab national project that entrenches the qualification of expertise, strengthens Arab capabilities in the vital space sector and makes it possible to build on data that moves the path in a new direction.
He continued: "There are undoubtedly anticipated scientific results, but whoever looks at the matter from afar realises further and more important visions: the UAE is laying the foundation for a serious renaissance in this field and proceeds according to a rising build-up of scientific institutions, a space agency, the launch of the Arab Space Cooperation Group, training talent and looking to higher academic specialisations that contribute to development."
Dr. Abdel Qader Abed, one of the founders of the Astronomical Society and professor of geology at the University of Jordan, said: "This is a serious and important step in the right direction. We in the Arab world had not previously achieved notable accomplishments in space science and astronomy, and we were at the bottom of the list. Since the UAE launched a specialised centre and continued through a scientific agency, we have come to look at matters with hope and a broader future vision."
He added: "Countries that harness their capabilities and pay large budgets for the advancement of science and reaching knowledge that benefits all humanity become an example to follow, and the UAE today opens this door and invites all Arabs to enter."
He continued: "Achieving this blessed leap calls for paying careful attention to what we as Arabs saw as distant and has become today within sight, and makes us leap towards Arab solidarity in the field of advanced sciences, achieving qualitative gains that benefit nature, the environment, astronomy and space, and thus humanity.
