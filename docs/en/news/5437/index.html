AUASS news · 3 October 2019

WASP-80: the star system 163 light years from Earth that will bear the names of Jordanian cities and heritage sites

Translated from the Arabic original. اقرأ النص الأصلي بالعربية

Brigadier Dr. Awni Mohammad Khasawneh, head of the Jordanian national committee for the vote on choosing Jordanian names for stars and exoplanets, announced that the International Astronomical Union has reserved the star WASP-80 and the planet orbiting it, WASP-80 b, to be named after Jordanian cities and heritage sites once the public vote closes on 24 October 2019. WASP-80 b is a gas giant exoplanet orbiting WASP-80, a K-type star. Its mass is about half that of Jupiter, it orbits its star in about three days at a distance of 0.03 astronomical units, and it was discovered in 2012.

The system lies in the constellation Aquila, the Eagle, a constellation known since antiquity that contains the stars Altair, Alshain and Deneb el Okab and can be seen from the northern hemisphere; its head is the star Altair.

Details of the star system

WASP-80 b is a gas giant exoplanet that orbits a K-type star. Its mass is 0.538 Jupiters, it takes 3.1 days to complete one orbit of its star, and is 0.0344 AU from its star. Its discovery was announced in 2012.
