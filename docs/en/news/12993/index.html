Lectures and events · 11 November 2022

The international astronomy conference concludes at Al al-Bayt University

Translated from the Arabic original. اقرأ النص الأصلي بالعربية

To mark the fiftieth anniversary of the last crewed spacecraft to land on the Moon in 1972, as part of a worldwide campaign by space and astronomy centres and institutes, jointly organised by the Arab Union for Astronomy and Space Sciences and the Asia-Pacific Space Cooperation Organization, with the support of Al al-Bayt University in Jordan and the University of Sharjah in the UAE, and with Arab and international participation, the conference was held in five sessions covering several main topics in the exploration of outer space.

On the second day, the participants continued the 2022 international conference "Space Exploration: The Moon and Beyond", organised by the university in cooperation with the University of Sharjah and the Arab Union for Astronomy and Space Sciences, with sessions on campus. Prof. Yu Qi, Secretary General of the Asia-Pacific Space Cooperation Organization, spoke about the importance of the conference and of advances in space technology for the service of humanity. In the second paper of the first session, Prof. Mounib El Eid, professor of astrophysics at the American University of Beirut, spoke about the importance of the James Webb Space Telescope in observing the first stars in the universe and how they formed.

In the second session, chaired by Prof. Mashhoor Al-Wardat and Dr. Osama Shalabiea, Dr. Saleh Al-Shidhani of Oman, member of the AUASS High Council, spoke about the Union's achievements and its future aspirations for studying the Moon. Mr. Jian Ding of China spoke about progress on the International Lunar Research Station (ILRS). Dr. Khaled Youssef Kamel, Vice Dean of the Faculty of Navigation and Space Technology, Egypt, and adviser to the Egyptian government's Fourth Industrial Revolution alliance, defined fourth-generation space technology, its importance for the international community, how global space agencies are fostering this trend, and its positive effects on governance, sustainability and the economies of countries that apply these technologies. He also spoke about the marriage between fourth-generation space technologies and the fourth industrial revolution, giving practical examples of the Internet of Things, artificial intelligence, 3D printing and digital twins and how they connect. He summed up the fourth generation of space as the era of the democratisation of space, in which the private sector plays a major role in researching and developing these technologies, governed by the laws and regulations set by each country's space agency in line with international space law.

In the second, evening session, chaired by Dr. Mohammad Shdeifat and Dr. Ali Nouh Al-Qudah, Jasmine Lazendic spoke about Mars exploration and Kazmina of Thailand about lunar exploration. APSCO's representative at the conference, Dr. Manop of Thailand, then spoke about space exploration initiatives and practices, and about space exploration in Thailand.

Prof. Mashhoor Al-Wardat, professor of astrophysics in the Department of Applied Physics and Astronomy at the University of Sharjah and coordinator of academic programmes at the Sharjah Academy for Astronomy, Space Sciences and Technology, spoke about exploration with the Hipparcos and Gaia space telescopes. He revealed a discrepancy between the two telescopes' distance measurements of binary stars, especially in Gaia's second data release, and presented a solution based on an analysis method he developed himself for binary and multiple stars.

Dr. Mohammad Talafha of ELTE University in Budapest and the Wigner RCP institute, a specialist in MHD simulations of solar cycles, discussed improved parameters of solar cycles using simulations of the surface flux transport (SFT) model of the magnetic field on the Sun's surface. Using the improved values of these parameters, he explained the role of quenching processes of the solar magnetic field and their relation to the solar cycle, especially its amplitude.

Dr. Ali Nouh of Al al-Bayt University gave a presentation on the importance of space sciences, through their main components (remote sensing, GIS and space-based crisis management), in supporting a state's capacity to confront disasters, mitigate their effects and deal with their consequences.

Dr. Nouh focused on the role of space sciences in forecasting disasters such as floods and forest fires, and on the need for the bodies concerned to join forces in building the digital models needed for this.

Mr. Imran Mohammad, director of the international affairs department at Pakistan's Space and Upper Atmosphere Research Commission (SUPARCO), discussed space exploration: past, present and future.

At the close of the conference, participants made several recommendations, including continuing to support such events regularly, annually or twice a year; strengthening communication between the bodies that contributed to this step, to make the most of this excellent beginning through the Arab Union for Astronomy and Space Sciences and APSCO; and supporting cooperation in preparing qualified human resources for space and astronomy through good education, training and joint research through the relevant institutions. The participants expressed their highest appreciation and respect to His Majesty King Abdullah II bin Al Hussein and to the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan, represented by Al al-Bayt University, for all the facilities and technical support that made the 2022 world conference "Space Exploration: The Moon and Beyond" a success. The head of the Asia-Pacific Space Cooperation Organization and the Secretary General of the Arab Union for Astronomy and Space Sciences, Dr. Awni Khasawneh, presented certificates of participation and appreciation to the participants.
