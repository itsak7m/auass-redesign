The five biggest space failures of 2019

Translated from the Arabic original. اقرأ النص الأصلي بالعربية

2019 is packed with space successes worth celebrating: China landed a spacecraft on the far side of the Moon for the first time in history, NASA's New Horizons spacecraft flew past the most distant object a spacecraft has ever visited, and a Japanese spacecraft managed to bring back samples from the surface of an asteroid, even firing a bullet at the asteroid to make an artificial crater. SpaceX also launched its crew-carrying spacecraft to the International Space Station… but without a crew, though it was still a step in the right direction, and the company launched the first prototype of the Starship vehicle.

But every big achievement is matched by a big failure; here we present the five biggest space failures of 2019. Steady your nerves!

1- The bankruptcy of Mars One

If we wanted to describe Mars One's failure in the most positive way possible, we could say it was just a naive dream. A more realistic assessment says it was closer to a fraud: a Dutch group managed to convince some investors to pour tens of millions of dollars into a plan to send the first human crew to Mars to establish a settlement there, that is, a one-way trip. In reality the plans seemed far from reality and poorly funded. Although the public relations campaign was excellent, the organisation was not working on developing its own space flight technologies, and simply assumed it could secure all its needs commercially. After the timeline changed again and again, no one was surprised when Mars One finally declared bankruptcy on 15 January.

2- The crash of SpaceIL's Beresheet spacecraft on the Moon's surface

SpaceIL was founded in 2011 to take part in Google's Lunar X Prize, whose prize was worth 30 million dollars, a competition for private groups to launch a robotic mission to the Moon's surface. Although the competition ended without any winner, SpaceIL decided to continue its work anyway, with direct government support. The Beresheet spacecraft nearly succeeded in landing, but crashed on the Moon's surface on 11 April. Everyone mourned Beresheet, and SpaceIL was praised for its efforts and its plans to try again. After that the story took a strange turn: in August reports said the spacecraft's payload included a secret capsule full of tardigrades (water bears), and that these microscopic creatures might have survived the crash and might be alive on the surface at this very moment. Now life may have arrived on the Moon's surface purely by accident. What a foolish mistake!

3- The explosion of SpaceX's Crew Dragon on the launch pad

After successfully launching the Crew Dragon spacecraft to the International Space Station in March, SpaceX was elated about its achievements. On 20 April the company was preparing for a standard static-fire test of the spacecraft's SuperDraco engines, but something went wrong, and the capsule caught fire and was completely destroyed. The company later found the cause was a leak in one of the propellant valves, and the explosion delayed Crew Dragon's schedule; as a result 2019 passed without any NASA astronaut going to space from US soil.

4- Iran's third consecutive failure to launch a rocket into space

Iran's space programme is still in its early stages, and this is clear in the three consecutive launch failures within one year, the last of which, in August, was probably the worst. In the failed missions in January and February the rockets failed to reach orbit, while in the third attempt on 29 August the rocket could not even leave the Earth: an explosion on the launch pad destroyed the small "Safir" rocket with its payload, most likely because of an accident during launch preparations. President Trump spoke about the explosion in one of his tweets, and many considered this tweet an attempt at provocation aimed at the Iranian government.

5- The crash of India's Vikram lunar lander on the Moon's surface

India was looking to become the fourth country in history to land a spacecraft on the Moon's surface. Its Chandrayaan-2 mission reached lunar orbit about a month before, and the Indian space agency was ready to send the Vikram lander to the Moon's surface at the south pole, and study the tiny lunar reserve of frozen water. But Vikram suffered a devastating accident, and as happened with Beresheet before it, it crashed on the Moon's surface and was lost for ever. There is no doubt that going up into space is hard, but landing on the Moon's surface is harder still
