Symposium: no conflict between Sharia and astronomy in determining the Ramadan crescent

Translated from the Arabic original. اقرأ النص الأصلي بالعربية

On Monday, 14 May 2018, Chief Justice Dr. Abdulkarim Khasawneh opened the fifth scientific symposium on "Astronomy and Sharia in Welcoming the Ramadan Crescent", organised by the Royal Jordanian Geographic Centre in cooperation with the Arab Union for Astronomy and Space Sciences and the Jordanian Astronomical Society. It was attended by the Grand Mufti of Jordan, Dr. Mohammad Al-Khalayleh, and the Mufti of the Armed Forces, Brigadier Majed Al-Darawsheh, with the participation of eminent scholars of Islamic law and specialists in astronomy and space science from religious institutions, Jordanian universities and astronomical societies.

The symposium, the fifth held by the Geographic Centre in a row, aims to discuss the conditions for determining crescent sighting on the basis of the relevant jurisprudential and astronomical principles, criteria and opinions. It is part of the Geographic Centre's scientific and cultural activities to spread astronomy and space sciences across society.

In his opening speech, Chief Justice Dr. Abdulkarim Khasawneh said that God created the celestial bodies and made their motion extremely precise and governed by an unerring order. Time, such as days and months, arises from the motion of these bodies, and Muslim scholars are able to determine the times of prayer, fasting, Hajj and other acts of worship; there is no disagreement between Sharia and astronomy in setting these times.

He noted that scholars of Islamic law rely on the opinion of astronomers in establishing the beginning of the months, sighting the crescent and setting the dates of Islamic occasions, on the basis of precise astronomical calculations that serve Muslims, and stressed that there is nothing wrong with using telescopes and modern observing instruments that help confirm the sighting of the crescent.

Dr. Abdulkarim Khasawneh thanked the Geographic Centre for organising such scientific symposia, which interest all sections of society and whose specialised topics include discussing the conditions for crescent sighting on the basis of jurisprudential and astronomical principles, criteria and opinions, especially as they bring together scholars of Islamic law and specialists in space and astronomy.

For his part, the Director General of the Geographic Centre and Secretary General of the Arab Union for Astronomy and Space Sciences, Brigadier Dr. Eng. Awni Khasawneh, said that determining the beginnings of the lunar months and the Islamic religious occasions that follow from them is of the utmost importance in the Islamic world, because of practical difficulties Muslims face today, such as the times of fasting and the Eids.

Brig. Khasawneh, who is also President of the Jordanian Astronomical Society, stressed the importance of astronomy, which has specialised tools such as telescopes, observatories and modern software that magnify tiny objects and bring distant ones closer, turning what was once imagined into tangible reality. He praised the role of the Crescent Committee, chaired by the Grand Mufti and made up of the best scholars of Islamic law and specialist astronomers from various state bodies, which works to reconcile astronomical calculation with naked-eye sighting in order to overcome disagreement, preserve the unity of the nation and heal its divisions.

In his speech, the Grand Mufti Dr. Mohammad Al-Khalayleh said that Ramadan is the month of worship and obedience and one of the great months Muslims look forward to, stressing the need to watch for the Ramadan crescent by both scientific and religious means, as there is no contradiction between science and Sharia on this matter.

Dr. Al-Khalayleh noted that Islam is a religion of knowledge, the religion of "Read", which is why Muslim scholars excelled in astronomy and relied on it. He highlighted the importance of this symposium, which brings together scholars of Islamic law and astronomers to discuss welcoming the crescent of Ramadan.

The Mufti of the Armed Forces, Brigadier Dr. Majed Al-Darawsheh, explained the view of scholars and specialists, including astronomers, on the role of calculation, and the value of drawing on pious and God-fearing specialists, noting that scholars of Islamic law should take the opinion of astronomers into account in setting out the legal ruling on the Ramadan crescent.

The scientific sessions then began. Dr. Mohammad Al-Aydi spoke about the astronomical and legal obstacles to adopting astronomical calculation; Dr. Mashhoor Al-Wardat presented a paper on daytime digital images of the new crescent and the jurisprudential views on accepting them as proof of the start of the month; astronomer Imad Mujahid's paper was entitled "The Ramadan crescent between legal sighting and astronomical calculation"; Dr. Abdelqader Abed's paper was entitled "Astronomy in pre-Islamic Arabic poetry"; and Dr. Abdulmajeed Nusair's lecture was entitled "Fast when you see it and break your fast when you see it: an enduring miracle".

On the sidelines of the symposium, the Crescent Committee met under the chairmanship of the Grand Mufti, Dr. Mohammad Al-Khalayleh, with specialists in astronomy, Islamic law and the Sharia judiciary from official bodies including the Chief Justice Department, the General Iftaa' Department, the Meteorological Department, the Geographic Centre, the Jordanian Astronomical Society, Al al-Bayt University and the Ministry of Awqaf, Islamic Affairs and Holy Places. The committee reviewed the astronomical calculations for sighting the Ramadan crescent in Jordan, the Arab and Islamic world and all parts of the globe, including the time of the new moon and the possibility of sighting it in each country.
