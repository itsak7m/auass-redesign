Lectures and events · 20 November 2024

Prof. Hamid Majool Al-Naimiy honours the participants at the close of the international symposium on "Advances in space communications"

Translated from the Arabic original. اقرأ النص الأصلي بالعربية

Under the patronage of the Chancellor of the University of Sharjah and President of the Arab Union for Astronomy and Space Sciences, Prof. Hamid Majool Al-Naimiy, the international symposium on advances in space communications, under the theme "Regional balance and challenges", concluded. It was held on 5–7 November 2024 at the Sharjah Academy for Astronomy, Space Sciences and Technology, United Arab Emirates, with a select group of experts and researchers from 16 countries around the world, as well as the UAE Space Agency, the UAE Telecommunications and Digital Government Regulatory Authority, and the CEO of Bahrain's National Space Science Agency, Dr. Mohamed Al-Aseeri.
The countries that took part in this important symposium were:
(China, Turkey, Pakistan, Iran, Mongolia, Peru, Thailand, Bangladesh, the United Kingdom, Egypt, Bahrain, Morocco, Sudan, Russia, Jordan and Italy).
The symposium was organised by the University of Sharjah and the Arab Union for Astronomy and Space Sciences in cooperation with the Asia-Pacific Space Cooperation Organization and the Islamic Network on Space Sciences and Technology, with the support of the Sharjah Academy for Astronomy, Space Sciences and Technology, with the aims of:
1. bringing together scientists, practitioners, researchers and experts to discuss and envision future developments in space communications in a comprehensive, global way;
2. providing a platform for researchers, practitioners and educators to present and discuss the latest innovations, trends and concerns, as well as the practical challenges they face and the solutions adopted in space communications;
3. providing a major platform for developing researchers through diverse international knowledge and expertise, improving the quality of decision-making by policymakers and promoting the best use of research knowledge. This was achieved through four scientific sessions over two days discussing more than 25 research papers, and eight training workshops given by leading experts and specialist researchers in the two days before the symposium.
The symposium also covered several important research topics in space communications, including applications of:
- space communications: new emerging trends, challenges and opportunities
- space communications and the role of intergovernmental organisations
- emerging trends in commercial space communications
- legal and regulatory issues in space communications

The opening session began with a speech by the patron, Prof. Hamid Majool Al-Naimiy, Chancellor of the University of Sharjah and Director General of the Sharjah Academy for Astronomy, Space Sciences and Technology. He welcomed the audience and participants and extended his highest thanks and appreciation to His Highness Sheikh Sultan bin Ahmed Al Qasimi, Deputy Ruler of Sharjah and President of the University, for his continued support and wise guidance. He described the symposium as a source of inspiration for experts and researchers and a valuable platform for strengthening cooperation and progress with international institutions in space science, communications and technology, with more than 15 countries taking part, and noted that the university offers several academic programmes in this field, such as the master's in astronomy and space sciences.

Dr. Xu Yansong, Deputy Director of the Asia-Pacific Space Cooperation Organization, said in his speech: "Effective space communication systems are essential for strengthening international cooperation, supporting scientific research and bridging the digital divide; the forum aims to provide a platform for sharing knowledge and expertise, cooperation and innovation in space", adding that such initiatives, symposia and scientific discussions, which share visions and explore opportunities for international cooperation, serve society.
In his speech, Dr. Awni Mohammad Khasawneh, Secretary General of the Arab Union for Astronomy and Space Sciences and chair of the symposium's organising committee, pointed to the importance of the event, which was preceded by a two-day training workshop entitled "Frequency and orbit elements in satellite design: ITU regulations and international standards". The workshop focused on developing practical skills and exchanging expertise in various areas of space communications and was given by experts from the International Telecommunication Union and China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology.

Topics covered included:
CubeSat design, the software used and the related control systems;
lectures on artificial intelligence for modern spectrum management and monitoring;
lectures on space stations and ground stations, radio communications and emerging technologies;
designing the technical specifications of communication payloads for satellites in low orbits, along with the problems and challenges these satellites face and the benefits they provide in many fields.
Dr. Khasawneh explained that the Union seeks to train Arab talent by organising conferences and events that raise the level of space and astronomy science in the Arab region. He stressed that space information and data of all kinds have become one of the most important pillars of human knowledge and a hallmark of our age: building a modern state now depends mainly on the availability of this information and data for sound planning for the future, as the basic resource for setting strategies and preparing development plans.

The symposium stood out for the variety of its lectures, activities and events, with important research presented on several topics, including studies on astrobiology and space communications, with a focus on bridging the gap towards interstellar exploration. Applications of artificial intelligence in space communications were presented, as well as lectures on the role of the UAE Telecommunications and Digital Government Regulatory Authority in regulating the space communications market and its challenges and opportunities. The symposium also discussed security risks and vulnerabilities in nanosatellite communication systems.

The symposium included entertaining science shows in the Sharjah Planetarium, the newest and largest in the Arab region, and a visit to the CubeSat design and manufacturing laboratory and the optical and radio observatory. Participants also joined an observing trip highlighting the details of celestial objects and exploring the planets and stars, with detailed explanations of the motions of the sky using telescopes. These activities are part of the Academy's efforts to promote scientific culture in space and astronomy. At the close of the symposium, Prof. Hamid Majool Al-Naimiy honoured the participants in appreciation of their valuable contributions.

Secretary General of the Union
Dr. Awni Mohammad Khasawneh
