24 words the ancient Arabs used for the hours of night and day

Translated from the Arabic original. اقرأ النص الأصلي بالعربية

The ancient Arab did not look at a wristwatch to fix an appointment or arrange an event that happened to him at some time of night or day; there was no AM and PM to determine that as now. Even so, the precision of fixing the times and hours of night and day among the Arabs from far-off decades was disciplined by (24) words, each representing a segment of time, listed by al-Tha'alibi in his book Fiqh al-Lugha wa Sirr al-'Arabiyya, with the meanings of these words given in al-Qamus al-Muhit:

Beginning with the names of the hours of the day:

al-Shuruq: the time the Sun has fully risen

al-Bukur: the beginning of something, here the beginning of the day after sunrise

al-Ghudwa: meaning early morning, a time between dawn and sunrise

al-Duha: when the Sun has risen, the rising and extending of the day

al-Hajira: midday when the heat intensifies

al-Zahira: the point of midday

al-Rawah: from the Sun's decline to night

al-'Asr: the time at the end of the day until the Sun reddens, also called al-Habs

al-Qasr: the mingling of night and al-Habs (asr); one says: "I came to him at qasr"

al-Asil: the time when the Sun yellows towards its setting

al-'Ashiyy: the end of the day, from the Sun's decline to sunset

al-Ghurub: sunset and the Sun's withdrawal from the day; ghurub is the start of night

The hours of night, in order:

al-Shafaq, al-Ghasaq, al-'Atama, al-Sudfa, al-Fahma, al-Zulla, al-Zulfa, al-Buhra, al-Sahar, al-Fajr, al-Subh, al-Sabah.

Did you know the names of day and night among the ancient Arabs? What do you think about that?
