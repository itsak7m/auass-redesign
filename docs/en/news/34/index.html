AUASS takes part in the Third International Conference on the History of Arab and Islamic Sciences

Translated from the Arabic original. اقرأ النص الأصلي بالعربية

The Arab Union for Astronomy and Space Sciences is taking part in the Third International Conference on the History of Arab and Islamic Sciences, held under the theme "Arab and Islamic Scientific Heritage: Creativity, Acculturation and Coexistence" on 5–7 December 2017 in Sharjah, United Arab Emirates, under the patronage of His Highness Sheikh Dr. Sultan bin Mohammed Al Qasimi, Member of the Supreme Council, Ruler of Sharjah and President of the University of Sharjah.

AUASS Secretary General Dr. Eng. Awni Mohammad Khasawneh will present a paper entitled "The Astronomical Heritage of Jordan", and several members of the AUASS High Council will also take part, among them:

1. Prof. Shawqi Al-Dallal – Bahrain.

2. Prof. Saleh Al-Shidhani – Oman.

3. Roger Hajjar – Lebanon.

4. Dr. Mashhoor Al-Wardat – Jordan.

5. Dr. Mohammad Al-Osairi – Syria.

6. Dr. Saleh Al-Saab – Saudi Arabia.

7. Eng. Khalil Konsul – Jordan.

The conference will discuss the following themes:

Theme one: The creative environment and its role in building a knowledge society in Islamic civilisation.

  • Features of the creative environment in the golden ages of Islamic civilisation.
  • Features of the innovator and reformer among Muslim scholars (yesterday and today).
  • Innovative ideas: showing how an innovative idea of a classical Muslim scholar can be used today, whether in the classroom (to improve a student's grasp of a concept or skill), in industry or in medicine.
  • Reports on research and projects serving the history of Arab and Islamic sciences: researchers and project leaders report on research and projects related to the history of science. Projects may be at any stage of implementation, so reports may focus on the project's concept and design, its progress or its final results. Papers and reports will be published in the conference proceedings.

Theme two: Scientific research in Islamic civilisation:

  • The history of academic chairs in Islamic civilisation and their role in spreading science.
  • Efforts of Muslim scholars to simplify science.
  • The role of endowments (waqf) in encouraging scientific research.
  • Contributions of Arab and Muslim scholars with special needs to the advancement of science in Islamic civilisation.
  • Orientalist approaches to the history of Arab and Islamic sciences.
  • Contributions of Arab and Muslim women to scientific research.

Theme three: Reviving the Arab and Islamic scientific heritage and linking it to the present:

  • The role of academic institutions and international, regional and local research centres in reviving the Arab and Islamic scientific heritage.
  • Information technology and modern communication in the service of the Arab and Islamic heritage.
  • Contemporary applications of the Arab and Islamic scientific heritage:
  1. Architecture and decorative arts.
  2. The tourism industry.

Theme four: The history of medicine and health sciences in Islamic civilisation:

  • Medical education among Muslims: its origins, development, curricula and teaching methods.
  • The role of endowments in advancing medical studies among Muslims.
  • Efforts of Arab and foreign scholars to uncover the Islamic medical heritage.
  • Pioneering physicians.

Theme five: Environmental sciences in Islamic thought:

  • The origins and development of ecology among Muslims.
  • Pioneers of environmental study in Islamic civilisation.
  • A clean environment in the thought of Arab and Muslim scholars (fighting pollution in all its forms).

Theme six: Architecture, decorative arts and crafts:

  • Pioneers of architecture, its arts and its engineering in Islamic civilisation.
  • Islamic architecture and ornament: influence and exchange.
  • Expressions of aesthetic and engineering genius among Muslim scholars:
  1. Religious architecture
  2. Civil architecture
  3. Urban planning
  4. Decorative arts
  5. Handicrafts
  6. Calligraphy and drawing
  • Applied handicrafts (metalwork, ceramics, woodwork, textiles and others).

Theme seven: Agriculture and irrigation in Islamic civilisation:

  • Technology and its role in the advancement of agriculture among Arabs and Muslims.
  • The study of agricultural pests and their treatment.
  • Efforts of Muslim scholars to develop irrigation methods and conserve water.
  • Efforts of Muslim scholars in the study of plants.

Theme eight: Basic sciences, astronomy and space in Islamic civilisation:

  • Astronomy between theory and practice in Islamic civilisation.
  • Rare astronomical manuscripts.
  • Mathematics and its applications in various fields in Islamic civilisation.
  • Chemistry and its industrial applications in Islamic civilisation.
  • The science of ingenious devices (ʿilm al-ḥiyal) and its industrial applications in Islamic civilisation.

Theme nine: The history of cryptology.

  • The origins of cryptology among Arabs and Muslims.
  • Pioneers of cryptology in Islamic civilisation.
  • Cryptology manuscripts (a bibliographic study).
  • The history of micro-writing in the Arab and Islamic heritage.

Theme ten: The Arab and Islamic scientific heritage and its role in promoting a culture of dialogue and coexistence.

  • Translation and its role in promoting cultural exchange between civilisations.
  • Scientific journeys and their role in promoting a culture of coexistence and tolerance.
  • The role of Islamic arts in promoting mutual understanding between peoples.

For more information, please visit:

http://www.sharjah.ac.ae/ar/Media/Conferences/ICHS17/Pages/vi.aspx

The sessions and activities of the third conference aim to highlight the role of Arab and Muslim scholars in spreading a culture of dialogue, coexistence and tolerance between peoples, a distinctive trait of Muslim scholars and one of the main reasons Islam spread across the world, especially in the Far East.
