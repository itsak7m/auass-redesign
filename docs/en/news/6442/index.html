Science news · 12 May 2020

A genome project and a hospital in space: world-class health care with national talent

Translated from the Arabic original. اقرأ النص الأصلي بالعربية

Source: Al Ittihad newspaper (UAE) | File prepared by: Sami Abdel Raouf, Maryam Bukhatamin, Fahad Buhindi
Editing: Ashraf Jibril
General supervision: Rashid Al Zaabi, Nasser Al Jabri
Design: Wael Abdel Majid

Some may consider it science fiction, but the country of the "no impossible", which has built an unprecedented experience over the past fifty years, is capable of fulfilling the aspirations of its medical sector to build the first hospital in space, and to employ the "Genome" project to provide the highest level of health care for its citizens and residents over the next fifty.
In the country of the "no impossible", which got ahead of its region and area into space, it is natural for the Ministry of Health and Community Protection to plan a fully virtual treatment system whose foundations are laid and run entirely by national personnel of the highest level of competence: a modern system in which the "robot" performs surgery remotely, the "Internet of Things" provides superb home medical care, and small "chips" implanted in the human body predict diseases and their nature.

A virtual treatment system
Dr. Mohammed Salim Al Olama, Undersecretary of the Ministry of Health and Community Protection, says: The UAE enters its second fifty with the world's first virtual ministry, to offer proactive solutions to the public, complete important national files and build predictive government systems for the future, through an unconventional virtual work system to re-engineer government services.
He added: The Ministry of Health looks forward to formulating future goals to advance on the international indicators related to health care, and to presenting a pioneering model in reinventing future health services that provide predictive solutions for patients. He pointed out that the future projects are built on health innovation and artificial intelligence, and support strategic and therapeutic decision-making, in addition to strengthening skills and building capacity at all levels, to provide comprehensive and innovative health services in line with global standards, to achieve the targets of the national agenda of UAE Vision 2021 and its centennial 2071.
Dr. Youssef Al Serkal, Assistant Undersecretary of the Ministry of Health and Community Protection and Chairman of the ministry's Artificial Intelligence Committee, revealed the 10 most prominent inventions expected in health care technology that the UAE could provide in the coming decades, as the ministry looks forward to its health services over the next fifty years being built on innovation and foresight, by applying the strategies of the Fourth Industrial Revolution, which in its health axis focus on robotic health care.
He stressed that the ministry will benefit from robots and nanotechnology to strengthen the potential to deliver remote health care and surgical services, and provide smart medical solutions around the clock through wearable technology, and without human intervention through smart services that reach the customer wherever they are. He announced that the possibility of implanting tiny devices in the human body is being studied, devices that predict the possibility of certain diseases or complications occurring, so that the treating doctor intervenes remotely using smart devices and robots without the need to transfer the patient to a health facility.
In addition, Dr. Kulthum Al Balushi, Director of the Hospitals Department at the Ministry of Health and Community Protection, announced that the ministry has ambitious plans and programmes to enable the latest health care technologies, the most important being artificial intelligence and blockchain, genomic medicine and telemedicine, pointing to biotechnology and nanotechnology for the space hospital, regenerative medicine and stem cell therapy, the use of robots in surgery and prosthetics, smart chips, home care through the Internet of Things, and 3D printing to supply medicines and medical supplies.
Al Balushi revealed that among the projects of the future over the coming five decades is implanting tiny smart devices that predict any risks or complications that may affect a person, and using virtual reality in treatment services.

Support for national personnel and incentives for rare talents
Health specialists confirmed that the country is moving forward in providing medical services according to global standards, but with continuous and rapid scientific progress the sector faces constant challenges and demands, which it handles through a strategy adopted by the Ministry of Health and Community Protection, aimed at developing the health system, improving quality, raising efficiency and providing the finest services needed by patients.
Dr. Mustafa Al Sayed Al Hashemi, Head of Government Communication and International Relations at Khalifa Hospital in Ras Al Khaimah, stresses the importance of finding practical solutions to the challenges facing the sector, so that it continues to provide the best health services and enable medical personnel to deal with new changes smoothly.
Al Hashemi pointed out that the biggest challenges facing the country's health sector are the lack of national personnel in some specialities such as doctors and their assistants, nursing staff and technicians, which leads to recruiting staff from abroad at large sums that affect the sector's budget. He pointed out that the second challenge is the lack of a proper balance between financial allocations on the salary scale and the medical labour market, explaining that there are rare specialities in medicine that should have special financial treatment, and stressed that finding suitable solutions to these two matters contributes to raising the efficiency and quality of the services provided. Dr. Khaled Al Zaabi, an internal medicine physician, says that the country's health sector is the one with the fewest shortcomings compared with similar sectors in the world, and despite that there are challenges that are easy to overcome, foremost among them reaching the highest level of efficiency and quality: it is necessary for all those working in the sector to be at the highest level of competence and training, from doctors to technicians, stressing that this goal is achieved through continuing education and training. He pointed to another challenge, the lack of primary care centres for the elderly and the concentration of those that exist in particular areas, explaining that redistributing them better contributes to providing quality services to this group, and lets staff gain experience and skills in dealing with them and learn their needs. Dr. Sherif Massad, consultant and head of the Paediatrics and Neonatology Department at Amina Hospital in Ajman, said that the health sector in every country in the world faces challenges, which in most countries are concentrated in a shortage of specialised hospitals; for example, it is necessary to dedicate a hospital for diabetes and a hospital for blood pressure diseases, pointing to the need for specialised centres to treat heart diseases and surgeries, and others to treat cancers of all kinds, with the mechanisms and equipment used for them provided. He stressed that there must be organ transplant, bone marrow and stem cell centres in the main hospitals, which would limit treatment abroad, while keeping up with the rapid scientific progress and the outputs of the technology revolution the world is living through.

Comprehensive health insurance and 24-hour emergency departments
Citizens and residents on UAE soil offered a set of suggestions to raise the health system, within the "Fifty" strategy, to ensure integrated health care for all patients and visitors, and spoke about their aspirations, suggestions and needs on the health side, foremost among them extending health insurance to cover everyone.
Citizen Ibrahim Hamad Al Hammadi says: Generalising the idea of health insurance is important, and helps ease the financial hardship on citizens, demanding like others that it be extended and benefited from in all medical facilities in the country. Amna Hassan Al Shamsi pointed to the challenge of too few neonatal incubators, which forces reliance on incubators from abroad, in addition to the lack of paediatric intensive care units in some of the country's hospitals, so families look for beds and vacancies outside the emirate.
Al Shamsi noted that there are no emergency departments working 24 hours in all primary care centres, and that there are too few ambulances in some medical facilities.
Citizen Amer Al Naqbi stressed the importance of strengthening the speed side and focusing on the importance of time for the patient, and working to reduce the set appointment periods, as there are still waiting lists in some hospitals, which pushes patients to look for other alternatives that may be outside the country, suggesting diagnosing the patient accurately before putting them on waiting lists and ordering patients according to the nature of the case and how well it can wait. Citizen Adnan Al Mansouri stresses the importance of increasing the number of primary health centres and activating their role more, so that they are the patient's first destination before going to big hospitals, as many patients avoid visiting these centres because they are convinced they contain only a general practitioner, with no examination equipment. Citizen Aisha Al Hammadi stresses the need to increase the number of specialised hospitals, foremost among them women's and maternity hospitals, especially in some emirates with a dense population of citizens. Citizen Ahmed Saif added: The problems resulting from increased population density must be faced, most notably the increased pressure on emergency and accident departments, stressing the importance of developing all emergency departments in all hospitals, or setting up separate accident and emergency centres in the big cities.

A lab to simulate medicine of the next 50
The Dubai Health Authority organised the first lab of its kind in the world to simulate the future of health care in the next fifty years and envision the progress and development the health sector will have after 5 decades. The authority put forward all the projects, initiatives and goals it has for discussion and dialogue in a brainstorming forum, and the topics of the exceptional health experience, sustainable living and space medicine topped the discussions.
The authority crystallised many innovative and creative ideas into a unified vision and a work agenda with defined items and tracks, to strengthen quality of life and support integrated health care systems. The lab saw continuous discussion and interaction among attendees around 7 main axes, which included an exceptional future health experience, life with sustainable health well-being, perpetual youth, a life without pain, the superhuman, space medicine, and the future of health economies.
