AUASS news · 26 August 2021

The President of Beni-Suef University in Egypt receives the Director of the UN-affiliated Regional Centre for Space Science Education

Translated from the Arabic original. اقرأ النص الأصلي بالعربية

On Tuesday, Dr. Mansour Hassan, President of Beni-Suef University, received Dr. Awni Mohammad Khasawneh, Director General of the UN-affiliated Regional Centre for Space Science and Technology Education for Western Asia, based in Jordan; Dr. Ammar Sakaji, Deputy Director for Academic Affairs; and Mr. Khaled Nasser Hussein, Deputy Director for Administrative Affairs, in the presence of Dr. Osama Shalabiea, Dean of the Faculty of Navigation Science and Space Technology.

The visit is part of activating the memorandum of understanding signed between the university and the Regional Centre on joint educational and research projects that help build a new generation of young scientists, researchers and students.

Dr. Mansour welcomed the Director General and his delegation, noting that cooperation between the two sides covers capacity building in space science and technology; satellite technology for remote sensing and its applications; space technology and artificial intelligence; space environment studies and astronomy; communications and satellite navigation systems; monitoring climate change; and information technology systems in space science and technology, as well as joint bachelor's and master's degrees through the relevant academic departments.

Dr. Eng. Awni Mohammad Khasawneh thanked the university president, the dean and everyone who helped make the scientific visit possible, praising the university and its unique faculties in the Middle East, including the Faculty of Space Science and Navigation Technology. He said the centre aims to run several educational programmes with Beni-Suef University, focusing on academic programmes in space and astronomy science, remote sensing and GIS, satellite communications, satellite meteorology and space law, as well as short, medium and long courses in these fields, helping teachers develop space and astronomy curricula and developing skills in satellite communications.
