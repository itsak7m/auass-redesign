2019: an exceptional year for the UAE space sector

Translated from the Arabic original. اقرأ النص الأصلي بالعربية

Nasser Al Jabri (Abu Dhabi)

The UAE's national space sector is the largest in the region, with investments reaching 22 billion dirhams, 10 satellites currently owned by the country and 8 more under construction, to be launched in the coming years, in addition to the foundations that have made the UAE a regional platform for space applications.
This year is an exceptional one for the space sector, after the success of the historic mission of Hazza Al Mansouri, the first Emirati astronaut, to the International Space Station, which demonstrated the knowledge and skill of the UAE's sons and their ability to carry out various scientific missions with full efficiency and capability, within the UAE Astronaut Programme, which aims to prepare and qualify Emirati astronauts to take part in space flights. The coming years will see more flights, in line with the goal of sustaining the programme and the continued participation of its personnel in future missions.
This year also saw the launch of the Arab Space Collaboration Group, the first space grouping of its kind, bringing together under its umbrella 11 Arab countries, with the aim of strengthening the Arab region's position on the global space map by launching a system that combines technical capabilities, qualifications and scientific personnel to work on advanced projects that reinforce the global scientific community's efforts to explore outer space, along with sponsoring initiatives and programmes to qualify and train personnel able to prepare generations of Arab youth who will push the joint projects towards their goals. The members of the group unanimously agreed that the UAE should chair it.
The group will work on completing the satellite "813", which will be funded by the UAE Space Agency and designed and built by a number of Arab engineers and young people from the countries that signed the declaration launching the group, in the advanced facilities of the National Space Science and Technology Center at United Arab Emirates University, the first space research centre in the Middle East. Developing the satellite will take a full 3 years and its expected lifespan is about 5 years. It will have a polar orbit at an altitude of 600 kilometres and will send data to a ground station in the UAE and secondary receiving stations in some Arab countries, to be used by various environmental bodies, municipalities and institutions concerned with the agricultural sector and urban planning.

The Red Planet

This year the country continued its efforts to complete the stages of the "Hope Probe", which will be launched in July next year and is expected to arrive in the first quarter of 2021, coinciding with the country's Golden Jubilee celebrations. It will collect information about the layers of Mars's atmosphere and study it to learn the causes of the loss of hydrogen and oxygen, the two basic components of water, from the upper layer of the Martian atmosphere. For the first time a space mission will also provide the international scientific community with an integrated picture of the Red Planet's atmosphere.
The efforts made within the "Hope Probe" included completing the verification of several aspects related to software, structure assembly, cameras and control. The probe was also confirmed to work with all its systems and components and to be able to communicate with the ground station, all of the probe's tests succeeded, and it entered the environmental testing phase, which runs until December.

The national strategy

In mid-October the UAE Space Agency announced the details of the National Space Sector Strategy 2030, which aims to strengthen the space sector's contribution to the national economy and to highlight the country's contributions in space at the regional and global levels, in addition to supporting the knowledge-based economy, creating an effective regulatory environment, developing specialised expertise, attracting top minds and stimulating creativity among young people, as well as building practical partnerships between industrial, educational and research institutions and strengthening international cooperation. The strategy will provide competitive, globally leading space services, strengthen advanced local capabilities in research, development and manufacturing of space technology, launch inspiring scientific and exploratory space missions, entrench a high national culture and expertise in space, and form effective local and global partnerships and investments in the space industry. These goals will be achieved through more than 20 comprehensive programmes and 80 initiatives.

Strengthening space investment

This year also saw the launch of the National Plan to Promote Space Investment, which aims to achieve the targets of UAE Vision 2021 and the UAE Centennial 2071, intended to make the UAE the best country in the world in various economic, social and environmental fields. The plan will contribute to achieving the Higher Policy for Science, Technology and Innovation and the UAE Strategy for the Fourth Industrial Revolution, by building a competitive national economy based on knowledge, innovation and future technological applications that integrate the latest physical, digital and biological technologies, as well as encouraging small and medium-sized entrepreneurship by attracting and boosting investment in the space industry.
The national space sector continued its efforts on small satellites aimed at qualifying human personnel. The satellite "MazenSat" reached its final stages before the launch expected in the first quarter of next year, built by Khalifa University students in cooperation with students of the American University of Ras Al Khaimah. After launch the project will measure greenhouse gas emissions such as methane and carbon dioxide, and the data and information it provides will be used by a number of ministries and bodies concerned with environmental affairs in the country. The distribution of greenhouse gas emissions in the atmosphere will also be studied using a short-wave infrared detector by a team of students specialised in monitoring, processing and analysing data.

The first space law

The space sector's achievements continued with the Federal National Council approving this year the country's first space law, made up of "54" articles, all aimed at setting a legislative framework to regulate the space sector and create a suitable regulatory environment to achieve the goals of the country's national space policy, by stimulating investment and encouraging participation of the private and academic sectors in the space sector and related activities, and supporting the application of the safety, security and environmental protection measures needed to strengthen the long-term stability and sustainability of space activities, and the country's commitment to implement the provisions of international treaties and agreements on outer space to which it is a party. The UAE has entrenched the concept of space diplomacy, with the number of agreements it has signed with the world's major space agencies reaching about 30. By expanding international representation and building the basic foundation of the space sector, the country has gained the global standing that enables it to exchange expertise and science, apply best practices and hone various skills, which is directly reflected in the UAE's various space programmes.

Developing research

The country continued its efforts supporting the development of research in the space sector. There is a group of centres specialised in space science and technology in the country, including the Mohammed bin Rashid Space Centre, the Sharjah Center for Astronomy and Space Sciences and the Space Reconnaissance Center, in addition to the Yahsat laboratory at Khalifa University. New York University Abu Dhabi is also working on establishing a space centre, which reflects the level of interest in space research and modern technologies.
