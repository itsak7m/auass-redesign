"The old woman's cold"

Translated from the Arabic original. اقرأ النص الأصلي بالعربية

By Prof. Dr. Majeed Jarad

I would like to mention some things to clarify the season of the "old woman's cold", as follows:
The old woman's days are seven days called (al-Husum) (beginning on 4 March and running to 11 March), and the cold in them is often severe, the winds strengthen and the weather turns gloomy. The season of the old woman's cold coincides with the season of "the madman sells his cloak", and it is called the cold of al-Husum by the Arabs, the old woman's cold by the Bedouin, and the cold of al-Shawla by farmers. The old woman's cold is the cold of al-Husum that God Almighty mentioned in His Book, where He said "He imposed them upon them for seven nights and eight days in succession", meaning the cold with which God destroyed the people of 'Ad. In the abridgement of Ibn Kathir's tafsir it is said that it comes at the tail end of winter, that is its end, so it was called "a'jaz" (rear ends), and the common people call it "al-'ajuz" (the old woman).
Al-Saba is the wind that the common people call al-Na'shi, that is, the wind blowing from the direction of the stars of Banat Na'sh (the Big Dipper) lying in the north-east, and al-Dabur is the north-west wind.
The cold of "the madman sells his cloak", or the old woman's cold, is applied to the cold at the end of winter, and this coincides with the end of the scorpions' season and the season of al-Hamimayn. A rise in temperature usually precedes this period, so some think the cold has gone, but it returns to attack again, accompanied by northerly or north-easterly winds. It is a sudden cold with an impact especially on children and the elderly, and winds laden with dust and dirt are active during this season. At its end warm southerly or south-easterly winds prevail, followed by the last breezes of spring, and then insects become agitated and allergies of all kinds increase. The cold of this season is destructive to crops because it usually attacks after a warm spell and unawares. Legend says that the reason it is called "the madman sells his cloak" is that a simple-minded man bought a cloak to protect him from the sting of winter, and at its end the summer warmth returned, so he thought the cold had passed and sold his cloak. The cold then came back with a fierce attack and killed him, so the season was named after him. Farmers call it the cold of al-Shawla, which is the tail of a small black spring bird called "Umm Suwayd" that appears in this period and keeps raising its tail high and then lowering it. The name "old woman's cold" goes back to an old woman in bygone ages among the Arabs who foretold the return of winter for a few days. They welcomed the arrival of spring and put on summer clothes, and the old woman's predictions came true, and the days were named after her. It is also said that there was an old woman who felt the warmth and sheared her sheep, then the cold returned and the sheep died. It is also said that an old woman had a she-camel that had not conceived at the start of winter, so she prayed to her Lord to send cold winds so her camel would conceive, and the winds blew that were called the old woman's cold. The old woman's cold is not cold every year nor warm all the time: it is mild in one year and cold at times, rainy and dusty from one year to the next according to climatic cycles.
