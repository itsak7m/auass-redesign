Science news · 2 January 2021

The main physics and astronomy discoveries, events and projects of 2020 (year in review)

Translated from the Arabic original. اقرأ النص الأصلي بالعربية

Happy new year 2021
Some of the most important physics and astronomy discoveries, events and projects of 2020 (year in review)

The coronavirus and its repercussions on space and astronomy sciences

The collapse of the iconic Arecibo observatory (the second-largest radio observatory)

Marked progress and acceleration in space missions towards Mars and work on setting strategies for crewed flights to the Red Planet

The beginning of the commercial space age

Development in the strategies of space settlement projects

SpaceX launches Starlink space systems and begins testing the resulting internet service across the United States.

Satellites and fifth-generation networks

Unprecedented results in bringing rock samples to Earth from different asteroids such as Bennu and Japan's Hayabusa, and the Moon (Chang'e 5)… etc.

China analyses strange materials from the far side of the Moon

The discovery of phosphine on Venus (phosphine is a product of biological processes as we know them, and this discovery falls within three scenarios: a product of biological processes, or of volcanoes, or there is a mechanism for producing phosphine unknown to us)

The launch of Solar Orbiter into space to study the Sun up close (NASA and ESA)

Protests in Hawaii over the Thirty Meter Telescope for religious reasons and beliefs

Farewell to the Spitzer Space Telescope

Welcome to comet NEOWISE

The Great Conjunction of Saturn and Jupiter on 21 December 2020

The discovery of quantum correlations in LIGO's massive mirrors (correlations between the microscopic and macroscopic worlds)

The detection of solar neutrinos from the carbon-nitrogen-oxygen cycle in the Sun. This discovery confirms the theory of the Sun's nuclear composition that has prevailed for 80 years and may be an attempt to solve the Sun's metallicity puzzle, a puzzle relating to the abundance of carbon, nitrogen and oxygen in the star

Unprecedented success in making the first room-temperature superconductor, at temperatures of up to 15 degrees Celsius, in a hydrogen-rich material under enormous pressure. Researchers believe it may be possible to reduce the pressure by changing the type of material.

Unprecedented results for quantum teleportation, a first step towards the quantum internet and towards a new era of quantum communications / networks

The experimental discovery of the polar ordering phenomenon (the phase transition of liquid crystals in the ferroelectric nematic) hypothesised by Peter Debye and Max Born in 1910

Successes using perovskite in thin films to create a highly sensitive X-ray detector, which may reduce the imaging dose

CERN announces the discovery of a new type of four-quark particle

A theoretical study linking the upper limit of the speed of sound to the fundamental constants in physics: the atomic fine-structure constant (alpha) and the proton-to-electron mass ratio.

Promising experiments in optics, especially scattering and diffraction phenomena with high precision in twisted layers of two-dimensional molybdenum trioxide. These experiments are related to the magic angle of graphene, and this discovery has important implications for nano-imaging, quantum optics, computing and low-energy optical signal processing.

The creation of a silicon-based material that emits light at suitable wavelengths for use in optical communications.

A successful scientific experiment showing how to use precisely mixed particles to treat cancer and monitor the treatment at the same time.

Anyons
Physicists braided strange quasiparticles called anyons, and this is the first proof of the existence of these particles. Theoretical physicists have long predicted the existence of anyons, a type of exotic quasiparticle resulting from the collective motions of electrons that behave together as a single particle. Frank Wilczek of MIT was the first to use the term "anyon" in the 1980s, so that we have fermions and bosons. And anyons (are a third class: but they will not appear as fundamental particles in our three-dimensional universe).

The discovery of material on Earth older than the solar system

The discovery of water on the Moon's surface

The puzzle of Betelgeuse's strange behaviour was solved

Solving the puzzle of fast radio bursts, and that they are not from alien civilisations

Gravitational-wave detection of the largest merger of black holes, which lies about 17 billion light years from us, and it is mentioned that it released radiation with energy equivalent to eight times the mass of the Sun.
