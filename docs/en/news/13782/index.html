Signs for those with certainty

Translated from the Arabic original. اقرأ النص الأصلي بالعربية

Let us begin with the noble verse "And on the earth are signs for those with certainty" (Adh-Dhariyat 20), then look into the other verses concerning the Earth. Only those with certainty seek out these verses, those who have combined knowledge with piety and humility, by the grace and mercy of God.

The verses are many: the number of verses in which the Earth is mentioned is 461, and it is entirely impossible to enumerate or count them in describing the Earth as a homeland, because we would go beyond the concept of the verse as a miracle that breaks the prevailing laws, known or unknown, to a principle simple in form and great in its becoming. For example: (1): "[It is] the work of God, who perfected all things" (An-Naml 88), which man cannot achieve the like of or imitate, though he may perhaps be given the ability to uncover it, describe it and analyse it. For man has taken up God's earth and it has become for him a settled place and a home. (2): "And you will have on the earth a place of settlement and provision for a time" (Al-Baqarah 36) and: (3): "God, who made for you the earth a place of settlement and the sky a ceiling…" (Ghafir 64) and: (4): "Is not He [better] who made the earth a place of settlement and placed within it rivers and made for it firmly set mountains" (An-Naml 61).

Settlement and stability: rooting, then growth, development, knowledge and learning, humility before God, worship, reflection and contemplation, work and striving, provision and livelihood, and everything God Almighty commanded. By this settlement and stability He intended, beyond the preceding meanings and others, a greater and further purpose in His words: (5): "And it is He who has made you successors upon the earth and raised some of you above others in degrees [of rank] that He may try you through what He has given you" (Al-An'am 165). The scholars are among those God raised through their knowledge, if it is joined with faith, and otherwise it is a gradual leading astray. The testing of people of knowledge in what they have been given is limited to uncovering the truths of the verses and glorifying them, in affirming God's oneness first and before all else. Whenever we read the words of God Almighty: (6): "Have they not travelled through the land and observed" in many places, among them Yusuf 109, Ghafir 82, Muhammad 10 and Ar-Rum 9, even without completing the text of the noble verse we are taken by a state of reflection and a fleeting moment of contemplation that then extends to minutes and hours, until we find a meaning that matches the breadth of this great signifier (the verse above). For this utterance begins with His words (Adh-Dhariyat 20) and ends exactly there. By way of field research and tracing the verses, it begins with the simplest terrestrial topographic variables and ends with the most complex physical, geological, biological and psychological phenomena, and in between all the sciences and branches of knowledge that took their sub-branch and specialisation from the diversity and difference of the verses, until studying and researching the meaning of God's words: (7): "And if you should count the favours of God, you could not enumerate them" in An-Nahl 18 became a part whose size cannot be estimated within (Adh-Dhariyat 20). Perhaps the number of favours and verses with God, glory be to Him, coincide and perhaps they differ: every favour is a sign even if not every sign is a favour by necessity or inevitability, and the distinction between them is easy in language but hard in comparison, in the manner of distinguishing between a human being's essence and structure and the composition and existence of the Earth, for example.

Despite that, there are common points that bring them together. Let us read together His words: (8): "And whatever He has created for you on the earth of varying colours. Indeed in that is a sign for a people who remember" (An-Nahl 13). Are the favours God created for us on the earth favours or not? The favour here is in the rank of a sign, and the sign is a favour. And in His words: (9): "Say: It is He who has dispersed you throughout the earth, and to Him you will be gathered" (Al-Mulk 24); (10): "And it is He who has dispersed you throughout the earth, and to Him you will be gathered" (Al-Mu'minun 79). Is what God has dispersed on the earth of a higher rank than man? God dispersed us on the earth and created us for a loftier purpose, in reality, than that for which the lower creatures were found. Among that is researching these verses and favours, defining their features and tracing their effects and forms, to strengthen faith with knowledge, and knowledge with faith, and this is one of the important ethical and value-based aspects of man's existence on the Earth as homeland, a home and settled place for him, with its exciting association with the universe and its contents.

It seems to us that the arrangement of the Qur'anic words for a purely stylistic purpose, in addition to the deep indicative meanings, is not easily attained without the two elements of the documented Arabic language and the inherited interpretation. But from another angle, there is grasping the intended meaning and its conditions. That is, the targeted meaning signified in the Qur'anic word may fall under the influence of the style of its presentation, and vice versa, within the framework of the eloquence of inimitability, and the inimitability of eloquence in the Qur'anic word itself, or in the Qur'anic text as a whole. This self-evident point may seem widespread and well known, but repeating it with the tip of the tongue is not like reaching it through hardship, like someone who couples the earth with the Pleiades without knowing what they are. Nor does he know at the same time the origins of formulating this expression among metonymy, metaphor and simile. We have achieved that many times before, as we face the words east, the two easts, the easts, west, the two wests and the wests, with the aim of extracting their meanings, and the first criterion on the subject rose, as it is the criterion that brings the meaning close to the physical and astronomical sciences in an objective and sound way.

* Director of the University of Sharjah, President of the Arab Union for Astronomy and Space Sciences
