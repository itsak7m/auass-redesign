Symposium: the Ramadan crescent between legal sighting and astronomical calculation

Translated from the Arabic original. اقرأ النص الأصلي بالعربية

The Arab Union for Astronomy and Space Sciences and Jordan's General Iftaa' Department held the symposium "The Ramadan crescent between legal sighting and astronomical calculation" on 19 March 2023, under the patronage of His Eminence the Grand Mufti of the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan, Sheikh Abdulkarim Khasawneh, and in the presence of Prof. Hamid Majool Al-Naimiy, President of the Arab Union for Astronomy and Space Sciences and Chancellor of the University of Sharjah. The symposium was moderated by Dr. Awni Mohammad Khasawneh, Secretary General of the Union, and many scholars of religion, jurisprudence and astronomy took part, giving specialised lectures on crescents and timekeeping, especially crescent sighting.

Opening the symposium, Dr. Awni Khasawneh described the great progress of astronomy in Jordan and the Arab world in recent decades, including the very precise calculation of crescent visibility and prayer times by specialist astronomers who have helped scholars of Islamic law and jurisprudence in sighting the crescent. The astronomer's role, he said, is limited to providing astronomical information to the legal decision-maker, not to establishing the beginning of Ramadan or Shawwal. He praised the Grand Mufti for taking into account the information of astronomers and specialists on the Crescent Committee of the General Iftaa' Department, which he chairs.

AUASS President Prof. Hamid Al-Naimiy thanked the Grand Mufti for his patronage, explaining that many observatories have recently spread across the Arab world, staffed by specialist astronomers who observe the crescents of the lunar months, especially Ramadan, Shawwal and Dhu al-Hijjah, which has helped unite the Arab and Islamic nation in seeking and sighting the crescent and overcoming disagreement.

In his speech as patron of the scientific session, the Grand Mufti, Sheikh Abdulkarim Khasawneh, said that God set specific times for the acts of worship of fasting, breaking the fast, prayer and Hajj: through sighting the crescent for the month of fasting, Eid al-Fitr and the Hajj season; through observing the motion of the Sun for prayer times; and through the stars for the direction of the qibla. He stressed that the General Iftaa' Department has a committee dedicated to crescent sighting, made up of scholars of Islamic law and astronomers from many official bodies, which meets at the start of each month to discuss the astronomical conditions for sighting the crescent.

The Secretary General of the General Iftaa' Department, Dr. Ahmad Al-Hasanat, said that the department in Jordan applies the texts of Islamic law in establishing the lunar months, including Ramadan and Shawwal: sighting the crescent with the naked eye or a telescope, supported by astronomical calculation that shows whether sighting is possible.

Sheikh Mohammad Hassan, adviser to the Syrian Ministry of Awqaf, said the ministry has issued Fatwa No. 7, which states that a witness's testimony must be rejected if it contradicts astronomical calculations: if a witness testifies to sighting the crescent when calculations have shown sighting to be impossible, the testimony is not accepted.

Dr. Mohammad Gharib of Egypt's National Research Institute of Astronomy and Geophysics then gave a talk on the differences in the beginnings of the Hijri months among Islamic countries.

We heard a lecture by Prof. Mashhoor Ahmad Al-Wardat, member of the AUASS High Council and professor of astrophysics at the University of Sharjah, on the crescents of the months of worship in 1444 AH, in which he showed that sighting the crescent at sunset on Tuesday would be impossible in all Arab countries.

Dr. Ahmad Al-Hasanat, Secretary General of the Jordanian Iftaa' Department, gave a talk on the Iftaa' methodology for establishing the lunar months, followed by Dr. Mohammad Gharaibeh of the Chief Justice Department with a talk on the crescent between sighting and astronomical calculation.

We were pleased to hear Eng. Mohammad Odeh, AUASS member and Director of the International Astronomical Center, who explained how crescent visibility can be predicted and confirmed Al-Wardat's conclusion that the crescent could not be sighted, adding that there was no need to look for the crescent at sunset on Tuesday.

Dr. Mohammad Al-Aydi of the Jordanian Ministry of Awqaf spoke about establishing the crescents between certainty and probability, and Mr. Hani Al-Dhulaie, AUASS member, spoke about the astronomical time of imsak, "Your fast is on the day you fast".

Dr. Anas Al-Khalayleh, Dean of the Faculty of Sharia at Zarqa Private University, then spoke about establishing the Ramadan crescent between sighting and astronomical calculation, followed by Dr. Yasser Abdel Hady of Egypt's National Research Institute of Astronomy and Geophysics on sighting the crescents of the Arabic months between astronomical calculation and visual sighting.

The symposium closed with kind words from the President and Secretary General of the Arab Union for Astronomy and Space Sciences, His Eminence the Grand Mufti of Jordan, Sheikh Abdulkarim Khasawneh, and Sheikh Mohammad Hassan Awad. Participants exchanged discussion, comments, questions, congratulations and good wishes for the coming of the blessed month of Ramadan.

The symposium lasted nearly five hours and was full of astronomical and legal information, enriched by excellent speakers keen to share knowledge in the finest and most precise way.

In such scientific and religious sessions, the meeting began well and ended well, and everyone agreed on the close link between astronomy and Islamic law.

In closing: O God, bring us safely to Ramadan, keep Ramadan safe for us and accept it from us, O Lord of the worlds.

Ramadan Mubarak to you all.
