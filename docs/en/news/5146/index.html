Did humans really land on the Moon?

Translated from the Arabic original. اقرأ النص الأصلي بالعربية

Source: Berlin – dpa Date: 15 June 2019

Let us admit that it still defies comprehension: humans flying to the Moon, driving a car there, and even playing golf.

The landing of the first of six crewed missions to the Moon on 20 July 1969 was a cornerstone in the history of humanity.

But 50 years after that landing, there are still those who doubt that humans were on this celestial body that orbits the Earth.

These sceptics believe that the pictures of the landing were taken in a studio. The following are the 10 main arguments of the sceptics:

1: A "fluttering" flag on the Moon

The "fluttering" American flag is the clearest example, in the sceptics' view, of a staged Moon landing, since there is no wind on the Moon, so the flag could not have fluttered.

However, the movements of the cloth held on a horizontal rod did not come from a breath of air but from vibrations of the pole on which the flag was fixed, arising for example when it was planted in the lunar ground or when it was turned, as Ralf Jaumann of the German Aerospace Center explained. Since the Moon has no atmosphere, it is hard to stop the flag's vibration.

2: Footprints in the lunar soil

Many pictures show the astronauts' tracks in the lunar dust. The critics ask: how can hard dust keep the shape of a footprint? Urs Mall of the Max Planck Institute for Solar System Research explains the good durability of the print by the cohesion of the extremely fine lunar soil. He said the components of this dust were never affected by wind or water and kept their edges, which made them stick well to one another.

3: The astronauts' jumps

The sceptics say that the astronauts' jumps as they moved on the Moon were exaggeratedly low, and that since gravity on the Moon's surface is so low, about one sixth of Earth's gravity, the astronauts' jumps should have been about a metre high each.

Mall attributes the low jumps to the weight of the astronauts' suits, each of which weighs about 85 kilograms, and the astronauts' inability to move freely.

Jaumann adds to this that the height of the jumps was not what mattered, and that the astronauts deliberately moved in small hops and short steps, for safety reasons.

4: Poor stability of the lunar vehicle

The astronauts drove vehicles on the Moon, and given the low gravity on the Moon, the vehicles should have slid off curves, according to the sceptics' theory. According to the American space research agency NASA, the vehicles' maximum speed was about 15 kilometres per hour, equivalent arithmetically, on similar ground, to the centrifugal force with which a car moves at 37.5 kilometres per hour on Earth with the same degree of turn. It is not at all likely that the astronauts took the curves at the vehicles' full speed.

5: Repeated "backgrounds"

The sceptics regard similarities on the lunar ground as evidence that similar backdrops were always used in the studio. But repetition in the pictures is not strange, as the astronauts took thousands of pictures from different angles at their landing site, as Mall explained, who also confirmed that the landscapes at the landing sites are naturally very similar.

6: Pictures without crosshairs

The lenses of the Hasselblad cameras used by the astronauts had a reticle, so as to be able, for example, to estimate distances better. These crosshairs appeared in some places to vanish behind objects, which the sceptics interpreted as compositing of pictures. On close inspection the crosshairs always appear to be there, but they are barely visible against the dark background, though according to Jaumann some pictures were published after later editing.

7: A sky without stars

No stars appear in the pictures the astronauts published of their Moon landing. The astronauts trod the lunar ground in the daytime, and the lunar day lasts two weeks of Earth days. The contrast in the pictures between the bright lunar surface and the dark sky was too strong for faint points of light to show in the sky.

8: Shadow angle not right

The sceptics see that there was more than one light source in the pictures they believe were taken in the studio, which made the shadows in these pictures run in different directions.

According to Mall, the reason for that is mainly the bumps on the lunar surface, which can make shadows look longer, shorter or distorted.

9: No crater where the lander touched down

There is no crater at the landing site, and almost no dust. What is odd about that? Jaumann replies, adding that the landings were not vertical but were approached from the side. According to Jaumann the vehicle's engines do not have the power to burn a hole in the hard rock of the Moon.

10: Deadly radiation

The mission crew members, on their way there and back, were exposed to high particle radiation from the Sun, especially in the Van Allen radiation belt that wraps the Earth. This radiation is assumed to have lasted hours and could have been lethal, according to the sceptics. But Mall estimates the time of crossing the Van Allen belt at about an hour, noting that the crew of the space flight was protected by the aluminium shell of the space capsule.

Accordingly, although the astronauts were exposed to high radiation, the radiation dose was limited.

NASA determined the flight path of the missions so that it could skirt the most intense radiation regions.

– After 50 years since this landing, there are still those who believe the landing pictures were taken in a studio.

– There is no wind on the Moon, and the American flag could not have fluttered without air.
