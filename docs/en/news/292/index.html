Astrology: which sign are you? – Dr. Hani Al-Dhulaie

Translated from the Arabic original. اقرأ النص الأصلي بالعربية

The Arabs never knew the zodiac signs; they used the stars only to travel through the desert in search of water and pasture. But they knew the motion of the sky, the seasons and the times of rain and wind, and would not move from one place to another until they were sure rain had fallen there. The stars of their sky were the lunar mansions of the Sun and Moon, 28 of them, such as the Pleiades, Aldebaran, al-Na'a'im, Sa'd al-Su'ud and Sa'd al-Akhbiya, and so on; the Sun stays in each for 13 days until the year is complete and the seasons are fulfilled.

The more urbanised nations of that age, however, were settled in their lands and depended on farming for their living, and astrology arose among them, especially among those who worshipped the planets or tied their motion to the will of the gods. All the stars of the sky keep fixed shapes and patterns except a few bodies that move among them, the wandering planets; some thought they were possessed by the spirits of gods and others thought they were the gods themselves.

For them Venus was the goddess of beauty, Mars the god of war, Jupiter the king of the gods, and so on for the other planets, which led people to believe they were seeing and calling upon their gods. If they could predict the motion and positions of these planets among the stars, they would be able to know what the future held, or so they thought, and astrology was born. Kings drew astrologers close, hoping to learn their horoscope and to protect their thrones and the security of their cities and kingdoms.

The astrologer's job was originally purely scientific and astronomical, known then as the science of "hay'a" (cosmography): computing the positions of the planets, the times when the Sun passes through the lunar mansions, and the retrograde motion and disappearance of planets in the Sun's glare, and recording these calculations in tables known as zijes, of which the best known in Arabic are the Zij al-Sabi' and the Zij of Ulugh Beg.

Although astrology contradicts the teachings of Islam, most of the astronomers whose names shine in Arab and Islamic history practised it and were close to the caliphs or governors of the time.

The sign of Sagittarius from al-Sufi's Book of Fixed Stars (Al Jazeera)

Although more than two thousand years have passed since astrology appeared, astrologers to this day repeat what the ancients drew of the positions of the zodiac signs and the dates the Sun enters them, not knowing that the sky turns and that the Earth has a third motion that changed every foundation of their knowledge.

Most people know the sign they belong to, and these signs, in order, are: Aries, Taurus, Gemini, Cancer, Leo, Virgo, Libra, Scorpio, Sagittarius, Capricorn, Aquarius and Pisces. 21 March is the most important date in this art, as it is the first day of spring, when the Sun is over the equator, day and night are equal in length, and the Sun is in Aries that day.

So anyone born in the period when the Sun is in Aries (21 March – 20 April) is considered an Aries. With the sky divided into 12 signs and the Sun in one of them each month, all people belong to 12 groups, which means that every half-billion of the world's current population share the same horoscope and the same astrological predictions. Why? Because, as astrologers claim, what they have in common is being born under the same sign.

If people knew the bitter truth they would feel quite differently about astrology. It is well known that the Earth orbits the Sun every year and spins on its axis every day, but it also moves in a third way that most people do not know: the axis itself wobbles, changing its direction relative to the stars in a cycle 26,000 years long. This motion is responsible for the position of the vernal equinox point, which when astrology appeared two thousand years ago was in Aries, which is why Aries had the honour of being the first sign in order.

As the direction of the Earth's axis changed with this motion, the order changed with it, so that in astronomy Aries is no longer the first sign; Pisces has taken its place, which means that everyone now called an Aries is actually a Pisces.


Symbols of the twelve zodiac signs (Al Jazeera)

When the International Astronomical Union drew the boundaries of each constellation in the sky in 1928 and set the dates the Sun enters each, astronomers found that a thirteenth constellation had entered the new division of the signs, Ophiuchus, lying between Scorpius and Sagittarius, where the Sun stays 18 days, while it stays in Scorpius for only seven days.

So the months were no longer divided equally among the twelve signs but varied according to how long the Sun stays in each. This changed all the rules of the game on which astrology rested; there is no meaning to a year of 13 signs, and no sanctity for Aries after that.

Worse still for astrologers: if we define the zodiac as the path the Sun alone follows, it has 13 signs, and if we define it as the path the Sun and all the planets follow, the number rises to 24. And what of the signs that would be added if we counted the path of Pluto or the newly discovered planets? Do they fall within astrologers' remit or outside it?

The puzzling questions astrologers cannot answer are many, but three are the strangest. First: do animals have a sign and a horoscope? Should a farmer looking for a guard dog ask for dogs born under a particular sign? Second: why is the day of birth taken as the person's sign and not the day of conception, which is the real moment for the influence of the heavenly bodies on the newborn?

Third: if astrologers are right that people born under some signs are better personally and as leaders than others born under less lucky signs, does that not mean the world should choose its leaders only from those lucky signs? Would that not be racism and chauvinism itself?

In conclusion, perhaps medicine has not yet made progress in finding a direct effect of stars and heavenly bodies on humans, but some studies say that psychiatric hospitals in some countries require staff to be on emergency duty on full-moon days in the middle of each lunar month, because some patients become agitated on those days.

But what every human knows for certain is that a single lamp left on in the bedroom will disturb someone many times more than the light of the Moon and all the stars combined would if that person slept one night in the open. So is it right, after all this, to speak of the influence of the stars on the future of humankind?
