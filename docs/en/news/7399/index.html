SpaceX begins testing Starlink space internet at 99 dollars a month

Translated from the Arabic original. اقرأ النص الأصلي بالعربية

At the end of last month SpaceX invited some users to join the public beta test of the Starlink programme, which aims to provide global internet coverage from space. To join the beta test, called the Better Than Nothing Beta, users must buy all the Starlink ground equipment for 499 dollars, then pay 99 dollars a month for the service.

The company sent emails to people who had signed up for the service through the SpaceX website to inform them the project's trial period had begun.

Speeds ranging between 50 Mbps and 150 Mbps

The email was brief, first explaining what beta users can expect from the initial Starlink system. The email said: "Expect to see data speeds ranging between 50 Mbps and 150 Mbps and latency ranging between 20 ms and 40 ms over the coming months as we improve the Starlink system.

There will also be brief periods of no connectivity at all." In the email SpaceX claims that latency and data speed will improve as the company launches more satellites and installs more ground stations to receive signals from those satellites.

The company also indicates that it will continue to update network software and expects latency to fall to between 16 ms and 19 ms by 2021. For the Starlink project, SpaceX plans to launch a huge constellation of 12,000 satellites in low orbits above the Earth in order to provide continuous broadband internet services.

The company claims that such a huge number of satellites is needed to provide at least one satellite above any region of the world at all times. So far SpaceX has launched about 900 Starlink satellites, although some failed to reach their orbit, and the company has also deorbited some other satellites.

Musk said SpaceX will need an additional 800 satellites to provide "significant operational capabilities". To connect to the system, users will need to buy SpaceX's own receiving equipment, which CEO Elon Musk described as resembling a "UFO on a stick".

In the new email, SpaceX says the user's receiving station, which includes a tripod to mount the receiver on the ground as well as a Wi-Fi router, will cost 499 dollars. The email provides a link for users to order the equipment.

In addition, SpaceX launched the Starlink app, which will help beta users set up their systems. Using augmented reality, the app lets users point the receiving station towards the satellites' line of sight in the sky.

The app also guides people through the process of installing the station. The app's description also indicates that the public beta test will begin in the United States and Canada this year.

Things have become less secret

During the summer SpaceX launched a secret beta test of the Starlink programme. According to code found on the company's website, the company told users that "you may not discuss your participation in the beta test online or with anyone but your family, unless they are SpaceX employees".

Despite the secrecy, the public got some glimpses of the system's speeds. Early Starlink tests carried out through the Ookla website showed speeds ranging between 11 Mbps and 60 Mbps.

During the launch of a batch of Starlink satellites, SpaceX claimed it had recorded data speeds that reached 100 Mbps. The company also claimed that it tested "space lasers"

on its satellites, which would let the vehicles communicate with each other in space and perhaps increase the amount of data transmitted. With the start of the public beta, things look much less secret, so early users may reveal more information about the performance of the Starlink satellites in the future.
