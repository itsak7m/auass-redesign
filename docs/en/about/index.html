About AUASS

The Arab Union for Astronomy and Space Sciences is an astronomy and space body headquartered in Amman, formally established in 1998. It brings together most Arab scientists, researchers and amateurs in space, astronomy and atmospheric science and technology, and in astronomical research. Since 2001 it has been one of the specialised unions affiliated with the Council of Arab Economic Unity of the General Secretariat of the League of Arab States, and it is a member of the International Astronomical Union.

It is currently led by its President, Prof. Hamid M.K. Al-Naimiy, Director of the University of Sharjah, and its Secretary General, Dr. Eng. Awni Al-Khasawneh. Its Honorary President is His Highness Sheikh Sultan bin Mohammed Al Qasimi, Ruler of Sharjah.

How it began

AUASS was established in August 1998 as an outcome of the Second Arab Conference on Astronomy and Space Sciences, held in Amman from 29 August to 1 September 1998. More than 100 astronomers and scientists from 14 Arab countries took part, along with observers from France, Italy and the United States. The participants decided to establish the Union with its headquarters in Amman, to develop astronomy and space sciences in the Arab countries through conferences, meetings, publications and joint research projects, in cooperation with international institutions.

Vision

AUASS endeavours to become a globally renowned organisation that unites individuals, institutions and organisations concerned with astronomy and space sciences, in order to make a lasting contribution to these fields and to ensure progress and development in the Arab world.

Mission

To raise the status of Arab and Muslim scientists in astronomy and space sciences, and to strengthen national and regional interest in these fields through education, research and space exploration, for the benefit of humanity's progress and development.

Fifteen goals in four themes

Our goals

Research and space science

  1. Raise the status and level of astronomical and space sciences so they play their part in advancing progress and developing Arab society scientifically and technically.
  2. Build capacity in satellite research and development to narrow the gap in space infrastructure.
  3. Build capacity in training professional talent to raise the level of space research and motivate development.
  4. Support scientific projects in space applications that help acquire and share the benefits of space technology in pursuit of AUASS's goals.
  5. Establish a fund to provide scholarships to Arab researchers in space sciences and astronomy.

Education and outreach

  1. Provide scientific advice on space and astronomy to institutions, bodies and individuals.
  2. Encourage interest in space sciences and astronomy in Arab countries and support scientific institutions and societies in the Arab world.
  3. Organise specialised conferences, seminars, workshops and exhibitions.
  4. Encourage member states to include astronomy and space sciences in school curricula, enrich the Arab library with astronomy books and periodicals, and encourage specialists to write and translate from reputable sources.

Arab and Islamic astronomical heritage

  1. Preserve the Arab and Islamic astronomical heritage and highlight its role in the advancement of human civilisation.
  2. Use scientific methods to determine the beginnings of lunar months, supported by astronomical observation.
  3. Encourage scientific research and publish a periodical devoted to Arab and Islamic heritage.

Arab and international cooperation

  1. Study scientific issues common to Arab countries and exchange information and experience in the basic and applied fields of space and astronomy.
  2. Establish a database of experts, scientists and enthusiasts, Arab and international, in space sciences, astronomy and astrophysics.
  3. Cooperate with international organisations and other Arab bodies in fields of common interest in astronomy and space.

Word from the President

Prof. Hamid M.K. Al-Naimiy

The Arab Union for Astronomy and Space Sciences: advancing our heritage in space and astronomy sciences.

Read the message

Twenty-five years have passed since colleagues, scientists, researchers, faculty members, specialists, Arab and Muslim enthusiasts in the fields of space and astronomy sciences gathered to establish a significant scientific and cultural entity: the Arab Union for Astronomy and Space Sciences. The Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan graciously hosted the headquarters of this union since 1998, and by the grace of Allah, it remains steadfast.

Today, as we celebrate the silver jubilee of the establishment of this blessed union, we not only feel pride and honour but also the privilege of fulfilling this national, scientific and religious duty as members of its supreme council. We worked diligently and faithfully to uphold integrity and dedication in the fields of space and astronomy sciences, inherited from our ancestors, Arab and Muslim scholars, who raised the banners of these sciences globally.

The Arab Union for Astronomy and Space Sciences is a specialised Arab scientific and cultural institution focusing on space, astronomy and atmospheric sciences, founded in 1998. Since its inception, it has made significant achievements in promoting Arab space and astronomy centres and their accomplishments. Its responsibilities have grown in the face of significant advancements in space and astronomy sciences globally, particularly encouraging the establishment of astronomical and space societies and clubs in countries lacking such organisations.

Despite these achievements, the responsibilities of the Union continue to increase, given the rapid advancements in space and astronomy sciences. Fortunately, many Arab countries have begun actively investing in these fields, establishing space agencies, astronomical observatories and space science departments in universities, indicating a promising future for Arab astronomy and space sciences.

On this occasion of our beloved union's silver jubilee, I express heartfelt gratitude to all my colleagues who have worked tirelessly to promote astronomical and space sciences in the Arab world and train youth in these vital disciplines. I wish success and progress to all and to our Union in reclaiming our historical and global status in these essential and beneficial fields for humanity.

And Allah is the Bestower of success and guidance.

Excerpts from the message published on the AUASS website.

Word from the Secretary General

Dr. Eng. Awni Mohammed Al-Khasawneh

Space science and astronomy have become important branches of modern science in the progress of nations and peoples.

Read the message

Space science and astronomy have become important branches of modern science in the progress of nations and peoples, and this field has received great attention and care from the wise and inspiring leaders of our Arab homeland.

The Arab Union for Astronomy and Space Sciences (AUASS) is one of the scientific beacons whose permanent headquarters is in the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan, and a starting point for disseminating scientific values and knowledge in space and astronomy.

AUASS was formally established in 1998 at the invitation of the Jordanian Astronomical Society, as an astronomical body concerned with space and astronomy sciences based in Amman. It has become one of the specialised unions affiliated with the Council of Arab Economic Unity of the General Secretariat of the League of Arab States since 2001, as well as a member of the International Astronomical Union (IAU). AUASS seeks to serve everyone, from students, amateurs and researchers to university faculty and staff, and anyone interested in space sciences and astronomy.

AUASS strives to obtain scholarships in cooperation with Arab universities and other countries, to qualify staff with scientific degrees in astronomy and space sciences, and to develop their skills through training courses, while encouraging research and publication in peer-reviewed journals.

The ambitions of AUASS are high: it strives to spread more astronomical bodies and institutions throughout the Arab world and to create a database of Arab scientists and amateurs as a reference for university students and amateurs alike.

Excerpts from the message published on the AUASS website.

Documented milestones

Our journey

  1. 1998

    Founded in Amman

    Established as an outcome of the Second Arab Conference on Astronomy and Space Sciences (29 Aug – 1 Sep 1998), attended by more than 100 astronomers and scientists from 14 Arab countries.

  2. 2001

    Joins the Arab League framework

    Becomes one of the specialised unions affiliated with the Council of Arab Economic Unity at the League of Arab States.

  3. 2012

    10th Arab Conference, Oman

    Sultan Qaboos University, 5–8 February 2012: "The Role of Space and Astronomy Sciences in Developing Contemporary Societies", with more than 500 participants from 39 countries.

  4. 2014

    11th Arab Conference, Sharjah

    University of Sharjah, 8–11 December 2014, alongside the Second International Conference on the History of Science among Arabs and Muslims.

  5. 2020

    Hosting MEARIM5

    With the IAU, AUASS hosted the 5th Middle East and Africa Regional Meeting on its virtual platform (10–12 November 2020), with participants from more than 37 countries.

  6. 2023

    Silver jubilee and 14th Conference

    University of Sharjah, 13–16 November 2023, marking 25 years since the founding, with more than 300 attendees and over 240 research papers.

  7. 2025

    MEARIM7 at the University of Jordan

    The 7th Middle East and Africa Regional IAU Meeting, 11–13 November 2025, under the patronage of AUASS and the University of Jordan.
